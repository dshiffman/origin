# Sheet materials on hand

Nominal sizes are labels, not dimensions. Plywood in particular is usually thinner than its nominal size, and it varies by sheet and supplier. Scripts take thickness as a parameter, so **measure each sheet with calipers in several spots and use the average** before generating joinery.

| Material | Nominal | Typical actual | Measured (fill in) | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plywood | 3/4 in | ~18–18.5 mm (often sold as 23/32 in) | | Bases, carcasses, structural parts |
| Plywood | 1/2 in | ~12 mm (often 15/32 in) | | Box sides, shelves, brackets |
| Plywood | 1/4 in | ~5.2–6 mm (often 7/32 in) | | Box bottoms, back panels, templates |
| Hardboard | 1/8 in | ~3.2 mm | | Templates, router guides, test cuts |
| Hardboard | 1/4 in | ~6.3 mm | | Jigs, templates, drawer bottoms |
| Particle board | 1/2 in | ~12.7 mm | | Shop jigs, prototypes (weak at edges and screws) |

## Notes

- Hardboard and particle board are usually close to nominal; plywood is the one that bites.
- Do a test cut in scrap first for any slot or joint meant to fit another part: cut, test the fit, adjust the clearance parameter, then cut the real part.
- 1/8 in hardboard is cheap and cuts fast, so it's the default material for trial-fitting a design before cutting good plywood.
- Particle board chips and doesn't hold screws in its edges well; fine for jigs and mock-ups, not for parts that get handled a lot.
